# Independent mechanisms of benzimidazole resistance across *Caenorhabditis* nematodes

Data, analysis scripts, and results from the article "Independent mechanisms of benzimidazole resistance across *Caenorhabditis* nematodes"

## Abstract
Benzimidazoles, a widely used class of anthelmintic drugs, target beta-tubulin, disrupt microtubule formation, and cause nematode death. In parasitic nematodes, mutations in beta-tubulin genes are predicted to inhibit benzimidazole binding and are associated with resistance. Similarly, in the free-living nematode Caenorhabditis elegans, mutations in a beta-tubulin gene, ben-1, are the primary drivers of benzimidazole resistance. The recurrent association of benzimidazole resistance with beta-tubulins suggests that clade V nematodes acquired benzimidazole resistance by mutations in beta-tubulin genes. To determine if beta-tubulin mediated benzimidazole resistance evolved across other clade V nematodes, we identified predicted resistance alleles in beta-tubulin genes across wild strains from three Caenorhabditis species: C. elegans, Caenorhabditis briggsae, and Caenorhabditis tropicalis. We hypothesized that, if these species experienced similar selective pressures, they would evolve resistance to benzimidazoles by mutations in any of the five conserved beta-tubulin genes (tbb-1, tbb-2, mec-7, tbb-4, and ben-1). Using high-throughput larval development assays, we tested the association of alleles predicted to impact beta-tubulins with benzimidazole resistance. In C. elegans, we found that only variants identified in ben-1 were associated with benzimidazole resistance. Although C. briggsae had variants in tbb-1, tbb-2, mec-7, tbb-4, and ben-1, only two variants in ben-1 were associated with benzimidazole resistance (W21stop and Q134H). In C. tropicalis, no variants in any beta-tubulin gene were associated with benzimidazole resistance. Our findings reveal a unique pattern of beta-tubulin mediated benzimidazole resistance for each of the three Caenorhabditis species and highlight the importance of defining benzimidazole resistance mechanisms more broadly across clade V nematodes.
